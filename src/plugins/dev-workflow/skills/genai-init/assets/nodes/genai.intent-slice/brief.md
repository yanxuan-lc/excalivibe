# Confirm the slice before design work is spent

Put the thin slice in front of the person and get a decision on **this slice**, not on the idea.
Design work is the expensive part and it is spent against whatever slice was settled on, so the
cheap moment to be wrong is now.

Show three things and nothing else:

- **What this change covers** — one paragraph, in their words.
- **What it deliberately leaves out**, and what that costs. A slice with no stated exclusions has
  not been sliced.
- **The first thing that would be built**, concretely enough to picture.

Record the decision, the slice as confirmed, and any condition attached to it. A condition recorded
but not folded into the slice is the same as no condition — the next step reads the slice, not this
conversation.

Only core changes come through here. If a person is being asked to confirm something routine, the
graph put a step where it did not belong, and saying so is more useful than rubber-stamping it.
