# Turn the topic into a question that has an answer

What arrives is almost always a topic, not a question. Investigating a topic returns a survey, and a
survey is what people read and then still do not know what to do.

Settle three things with the person, and write them down:

- **What would count as an answer.** Not "learn about X" — "know whether we can do X with Y, and
  what it costs". If nobody can say what an answer looks like, the research has no finishing line.
- **What decision this changes.** Research that changes no decision is reading, and reading does not
  need a flow. If there is no decision, say so rather than proceeding.
- **What is out of scope**, and why. This is the part that keeps a two-day investigation from
  becoming a two-week one.

**No findings here.** Not even the ones you already have. This step establishes the question; a
finding recorded now is a finding nobody planned to look for and nobody will check the provenance of.
